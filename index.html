🚨 Breaches & Active Threats
Critical

N-able says attackers took over N-central RMM servers after its first fix didn't hold

📅 Aug 2, 2026

N-able disclosed that attackers exploited an authentication-bypass chain (CVE-2026-18556, then a bypass of the initial fix tracked as CVE-2026-18577) to gain unauthenticated administrative access to N-central servers, then abused the platform's built-in Take Control feature to pivot into managed customer endpoints and deploy Cloudflare tunnels for persistence. N-able shipped a second hotfix, build 2026.3.1.7, on August 2 and is urging every customer to upgrade immediately.

Why it matters: This is an RMM supply-chain compromise with a direct path into every endpoint an affected MSP manages — if you run N-central, confirm you're on 2026.3.1.7 (not just the June fix) and check Take Control audit logs for unrecognized sessions.

Notable

Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center zero-day exploited via hard-coded credentials

📅 Jul 29, 2026

Cisco's PSIRT confirmed active exploitation of CVE-2026-20316, caused by static credentials for a low-privilege account built into Secure FMC's web interface, reported by Jimi Sebree of Horizon3.ai. The CVSS score is only 5.3, but Cisco rated it High Impact because the access can be chained with other FMC flaws for privilege escalation; there's no workaround, and Cisco is telling admins to rotate all credentials, keys and certificates on affected appliances after patching.

Why it matters: A "medium-severity" score understates the risk here — check FMC logs for entries referencing /var/tmp/license.tmp (a reported exploitation indicator) and apply the hotfix even if the CVSS number looks low-priority on your patch queue.

Two-minute Microsoft Teams vishing calls led to Chaos ransomware in under 17 hours

📅 Jul 30, 2026

Sophos tracked a campaign, STAC4749, that hit dozens of North American organizations between February and June 2026 using brief Teams voice calls impersonating IT helpdesk staff to talk employees into granting remote access via Microsoft Quick Assist. Why it matters: Seventeen hours from a two-minute phone call to full encryption leaves almost no window for manual detection — treat any unsolicited Teams "IT support" call as suspicious by default and verify through an independent channel before anyone grants remote-access permissions.

🛡️ Vulnerabilities & Patches
Critical

Broadcom patches twin CVSS 9.8 VMware vCenter auth-bypass and RCE flaws

📅 Jul 29, 2026

Advisory VMSA-2026-0006 covers CVE-2026-59309, an authentication-bypass flaw in VMware Directory Service letting a network-adjacent attacker fully bypass vCenter login, and CVE-2026-59310, a directory-traversal bug in vCenter's Syslog server enabling remote code execution — both scored 9.8 with no workaround. A related flaw, CVE-2026-47876 (CVSS 9.3), lets a malicious VM guest escape via the VMXNET3 virtual adapter to execute code on the ESXi host itself. Broadcom has released patched builds across vCenter and ESXi branches (9.1.0.0300, 9.0.2.0100, 8.0 U3k) and reports no evidence of exploitation so far.

Why it matters: No workaround exists for the traversal bug, so any internet- or intranet-exposed vCenter instance should be prioritized for patching now, before "no evidence of exploitation" changes.

Notable

CosmosEscape flaw could have handed attackers a key to every Azure Cosmos DB database

📅 Jul 30, 2026

Wiz Research found that Azure Cosmos DB's Gremlin query engine translated graph queries into executable .NET code with insufficient sandboxing, letting crafted queries escape the sandbox and extract an unscoped "Cosmos Master Key" capable of retrieving the primary key for any tenant's database across SQL, MongoDB, Cassandra and Gremlin APIs. Because Cosmos DB underpins Microsoft Entra ID, Teams and Copilot, the exposure reached into Microsoft's own backend; Microsoft deployed a 48-hour hotfix after the November 2025 report and completed a full architectural fix — eliminating the master key entirely — in July 2026, finding no evidence of exploitation beyond the researchers' own testing.

Why it matters: No customer action is required, but it's a reminder that a single platform-wide secret behind a managed cloud service can cascade into the vendor's own identity and productivity stack — worth asking any cloud provider what a "master key" architecture looks like for services you depend on.
Source
Notable

20-year-old IPMI flaw still exposes 36,872 data-center management controllers

📅 Jul 31, 2026

LavaHQ researchers found that CVE-2013-4786, a flaw in the IPMI 2.0 Remote Authenticated Key-Exchange Protocol, still exposes 36,872 internet-facing Baseboard Management Controllers, of which 24,650 leak password-derived data before login, enabling offline password cracking. Supermicro's predictable factory-password format lets a modern GPU rig brute-force the full keyspace in about an hour, and researchers found active exploitation, including an HPE iLO interface displaying a 0.3 BTC ransom note.

Why it matters: BMCs run below the OS and are invisible to most endpoint security tools, so a compromise survives OS reinstalls — remove IPMI from the public internet, block UDP port 623, and rotate factory-issued BMC passwords today, not on the next patch cycle.

📅 Jul 30, 2026

Why it matters: Two of the three affected organizations didn't know they'd been accessed until Anthropic told them — a second frontier lab confirming the same failure mode as OpenAI's Hugging Face incident in the same week means "the vendor's sandbox is isolated" is now a claim worth verifying independently, not assuming.

Self-propagating prompt-injection "AI worm" found in Microsoft Word Copilot

📅 Aug 1, 2026

Researcher EN Klype Salt disclosed that hidden, invisibly formatted prompts embedded in a Word document are fully parsed by Copilot's underlying LLM, letting attacker instructions cross the trust boundary to silently manipulate active content — such as halving financial figures in a report. Copilot then copies the malicious prompt into the edited document using concealed formatting, so any downstream reuse re-triggers the attack across SharePoint, Teams and email. The behavior reproduced across multiple Copilot configurations despite a 144-day coordinated disclosure with Microsoft's MSRC; only partial fixes exist.

Why it matters: A vulnerability class that survives 144 days of vendor coordination and still only has a partial fix means the mitigation is on your side for now — treat externally sourced documents as untrusted before any AI-assisted editing, and manually review Copilot-generated content before it's reused or forwarded.

Why it matters: Detecting a planet through its atmospheric signature, not just its light, is a technique that could transform how astronomers uncover worlds hidden in the glare of their stars.
