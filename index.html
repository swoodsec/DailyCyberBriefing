🚨 Breaches & Active Threats
Critical

Clop launches data-theft extortion campaign against PTC Windchill and FlexPLM

📅 Jul 24, 2026

Clop (Cl0p) is exploiting CVE-2026-12569, a critical unauthenticated deserialization flaw in PTC's Windchill and FlexPLM product-lifecycle-management platforms, to deploy JSP webshells and exfiltrate product design data. ReliaQuest reports active exploitation across aerospace, automotive, defense and retail customers; Clop has begun emailing victims from new cryptohox.com extortion addresses, its long-standing pattern before publishing stolen files.

Why it matters: PLM platforms hold a company's actual product IP, not just business records — if you run Windchill or FlexPLM, patch immediately, place the servers behind a VPN or access gateway, and treat any suspected compromise as a design-theft incident, not just a data breach.

Critical

Russian "Laundry Bear" spies exploited Zimbra zero-click flaw for a year to steal mail and 2FA codes

📅 Jul 23, 2026

NSA, CISA and partner agencies confirmed that a Russian state-backed group (tracked as Laundry Bear, Void Blizzard, CL-STA-1114 and TA488) exploited CVE-2025-66376, a stored-XSS zero-click flaw in Zimbra's Classic UI, since at least July 2025 to steal 90 days of mail, browser-saved passwords, 2FA scratch codes and directory listings from government, defense and financial targets across NATO, Ukraine and the US. The payload also mints an app-specific password that survives a victim's password reset.

Why it matters: Patching Zimbra closes the hole but not the account — any mailbox that opened a matching message in a vulnerable session should have its password reset, sessions invalidated and 2FA scratch codes regenerated, since the app-specific credential the malware creates outlives a routine password change.

🛡️ Vulnerabilities & Patches
Critical

"Certighost" exploit lets any Active Directory user impersonate a Domain Controller

📅 Jul 24, 2026

Researchers H0j3n and Aniq Fakhrul published a working exploit for CVE-2026-54121, a flaw in an AD CS certificate-enrollment fallback ("chase") that lets a normal domain account obtain a Domain Controller certificate and authenticate as that machine via PKINIT — then pull the krbtgt secret through DCSync. Microsoft patched the flaw (CVSS 8.8) on July 14; exploitation needs only network access and a domain account, no admin rights or user interaction, and no evidence of in-the-wild exploitation had surfaced as of publication.

Why it matters: This is a full-domain-compromise path from an ordinary user account with a public working exploit now available — if you run an Enterprise CA, install Microsoft's July 14 update on AD CS hosts today rather than waiting for your normal patch cycle.
Source

🤖 AI Security
Critical

Hacker runs open-source Hermes AI agent unattended for post-exploitation at Thai Finance Ministry

📅 Jul 24, 2026

An intruder already inside Thailand's Ministry of Finance network pointed the open-source Hermes AI agent at it with approval prompts disabled ("YOLO mode"), letting it autonomously run privilege-escalation scans, hunt for elevated-permission files, and crawl a personnel-records folder dating to 2012 — without a human approving each command. The operator left the agent's own logs on an open web directory, where Hunt.io and researcher Bob Diachenko found 585 files and 470MB of attack tooling, including custom kernel-exploit scripts and a Hadoop database service left accepting any password by default.

Why it matters: Unlike prior AI-assisted attacks where a vendor could ban the account, Hermes ran locally with no vendor watching and no kill switch — treat "AI agent in YOLO mode" as a distinct detection target and alert on web servers reaching internal Hadoop ports or a HermesWebUI response header, both of which this operator's setup exposed.

Notable

"AgentForger" flaw let one phishing link forge a persistent rogue agent inside ChatGPT Workspace

📅 Jul 23, 2026

Zenity Labs found that OpenAI's ChatGPT Agent Builder accepted an initialization prompt via URL parameter and auto-executed it, so a single crafted link — clicked by a logged-in employee with a connected app like Outlook or Slack — could silently create, configure with approvals switched off, and schedule an hourly-running AI agent under the victim's identity. The forged agent then took new instructions via emails tagged "TASK" from the attacker, harvesting documents and credentials and impersonating the victim in Teams phishing. OpenAI fixed the CSRF flaw June 8, before Zenity's public disclosure.

Why it matters: This is a cross-site request forgery attack that spawns an autonomous, self-scheduling AI insider rather than just stealing a session — audit which of your enterprise AI agent-builder tools accept state through URL parameters, since that pattern generalizes well beyond ChatGPT.

SAXON Q launches room-temperature diamond quantum computers

📅 Jul 21, 2026

German firm SAXON Q announced commercial availability of its 128- and 512-qubit diamond NV-center systems — said to be the first such machines to exceed 10 qubits — that run at room temperature with no cryogenic cooling or vacuum equipment. A proprietary sulfur co-implantation process reportedly yields greater than 85% qubit conversion and up to 99.92% gate fidelity.

Why it matters: Room-temperature, facility-free quantum hardware could put quantum experimentation within reach of ordinary labs and even mobile settings, sidestepping the costly cryogenics that gate most competing approaches.
