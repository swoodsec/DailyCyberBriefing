Microsoft and Meta report earnings today — with AI capex, not revenue, the number that moves the stocks

📅 Jul 29, 2026

Both companies report after the close today in the busiest stretch of earnings season, with Microsoft's fiscal Q4 pinned to Azure growth and Meta's Q2 revenue expected up about 27% to roughly $60B. Both enter with beats largely priced in, so the market-mover is forward capex: Meta has already lifted its 2026 spending outlook to $125B–$145B, and any surprise on spending guidance can swing the stock more than the earnings beat itself.

Why it matters: The megacaps are now graded as much on how aggressively they are spending on AI infrastructure as on what they earn, and today's prints set the tone for whether the buildout is seen as a growth engine or a cash-flow drain.

Apple touches a $5 trillion market cap for the first time

📅 Jul 28, 2026

Apple shares hit a session high that briefly pushed its market value to about $5.04 trillion, making it only the second company ever — after Nvidia — to reach the milestone, before pulling back below the line into the close. The run comes less than a year after Apple crossed $4 trillion, with the stock up roughly 24% year to date on strong iPhone demand.

Why it matters: Apple's climb rewards a deliberate strategy of sitting out the costly AI data-center arms race that has pressured cash flow at its peers — a striking counterpoint as the capex-heavy hyperscalers report this week.

China's CXMT surges 466% in Shanghai debut, becoming the most valuable A-share company

📅 Jul 27, 2026

Memory maker ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) jumped 466% on its first day on Shanghai's STAR Market after raising about $8.6 billion in Asia's biggest IPO of 2026, closing at a valuation near $488 billion. CXMT is the world's fourth-largest DRAM maker and held roughly 7.7% of the global DRAM market in 2025.

Why it matters: A blockbuster debut for China's leading domestic DRAM champion signals Beijing's push for memory-chip self-sufficiency is gaining real market momentum — and adds a new competitive threat for Micron, Samsung, and SK Hynix.

Paramount agrees to delay its Warner Bros. Discovery takeover until as late as June 2027

📅 Jul 24, 2026

Paramount Skydance stipulated that its roughly $110B acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery will not close until five days after a trial on state antitrust claims or June 1, 2027 — whichever comes first — after a California-led coalition of 12 states sued to block it. The delay means Paramount will begin incurring a $7-million-a-day "ticking fee," potentially $1.7B, while the deal sits in limbo.

Why it matters: Even with DOJ and EU clearance in hand, state attorneys general can now stall a media megamerger for the better part of a year, underscoring how state-level antitrust has become a real gate on big consolidation.

Apple launches a Klarna-backed "Apple Upgrade" leasing program starting at $17.99 a month

📅 Jul 28, 2026

Apple's new U.S. leasing program, run with Klarna, lets customers lease an iPhone for up to two years from $17.99 a month after a soft credit check, with Apple Watch, Mac, and iPad options too. Devices are returned at term end — or bought out or traded up — with no security deposit, replacing Apple's long-running iPhone Upgrade Program.

Why it matters: Swapping installment financing for outright leasing deepens Apple's push to make hardware a recurring subscription, tightening upgrade cycles and locking users further into its ecosystem.

📅 Jul 28, 2026

Microsoft's optional KB5101684 preview (builds 26200/26100.8973) rolls out the marquee features of the July cycle, including Point-in-time Restore — a Volume Shadow Copy-based rollback to a prior good state — plus Shared Audio for multiple Bluetooth outputs, a Screen Tint accessibility option, and calendar-based update-pause controls.

📅 Jul 27, 2026

Nvidia gathered a broad roster — including Microsoft, IBM, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Hugging Face, Red Hat, and the Linux Foundation — to build and share open-source tools for defending AI systems, extending existing OpenSSF and Linux Foundation efforts. Boeing's next Starliner flight slips to no earlier than Q4 as it works with NASA on a cargo mission

📅 Jul 28, 2026

In a July 28 SEC filing and earnings call, Boeing said it now expects an uncrewed Starliner cargo flight to the ISS no earlier than the fourth quarter of 2026, with CEO Kelly Ortberg calling the fixed-price program one the company is still working to get on track after cost overruns. Update

NASA astronaut and two cosmonauts return to Earth after 241 days on the ISS

📅 Jul 26, 2026

Chris Williams and Roscosmos crewmates Sergey Kud-Sverchkov and Sergei Mikaev landed their Soyuz MS-28 in Kazakhstan on July 26, wrapping an eight-month mission that orbited Earth 3,856 times and covered more than 102 million miles. It was the first spaceflight for Williams and Mikaev.

Why it matters: Routine long-duration crew rotations underscore how the aging ISS still anchors continuous human presence in orbit even as NASA plans its transition to commercial stations later this decade.

Update

Astronomers detect the first atmosphere on a rocky planet in a star's habitable zone

📅 Jul 17, 2026

A Harvard-led team reported in Science that helium is escaping the upper atmosphere of LHS 1140 b, a super-Earth 48 light-years away, confirming it holds a real, persistent atmosphere that has survived more than three billion years. The planet orbits a red dwarf within the habitable zone and is about 1.7 times Earth's radius.

Why it matters: The first solid evidence that a rocky, potentially temperate world can hang onto an atmosphere around a red dwarf is a milestone in the hunt for habitable Earth-like planets — and a prime target for follow-up biosignature searches.

Teleoperated humanoid robots perform surgeries in a medical first

📅 Jul 9, 2026

UC San Diego researchers used two teleoperated humanoid robots to complete gallbladder-removal surgeries on large mammals in a preclinical trial published in Nature — including one procedure done by two humanoids working side by side. Researchers say the platforms are cheaper and far more space-efficient than dedicated systems like Intuitive's da Vinci, though slower and less precise for now.

Why it matters: If versatile humanoids can eventually stand in for room-sized surgical robots, they could extend precision surgery to rural clinics, battlefields, and other settings that can't afford specialized operating suites.
