SpaceX's first earnings since its record IPO: revenue beat, but AI spending unnerves Wall Street

📅 Aug 4, 2026

In its debut quarterly report as a public company (NASDAQ: SPCX), SpaceX posted revenue of $7.81B, up 92% year over year and ahead of the ~$6.9B expected, while narrowing its net loss to $541M from $1B. But capital expenditures jumped more than sixfold to $18.37B — $15.83B of it on AI — sending the stock down about 8% after hours. CFO Bret Johnsen said the now-three-segment company (space, Starlink connectivity, and AI) is on pace for $100B in annualized recurring revenue by year-end.

Why it matters: This is the market's first audited look inside SpaceX after June's $85.7B IPO — the largest in history — and the sell-off shows the same "capex vigilante" scrutiny hammering hyperscalers now applies to Elon Musk's rocket-and-AI empire.

AMD's revenue climbs 50% and data-center sales double — but the stock falls

📅 Aug 4, 2026

AMD reported Q2 revenue of $11.5B, up 50%, with data-center sales up 107% to $6.7B — now 58% of the company — as EPYC CPUs and Instinct GPUs both surged. CEO Lisa Su guided Q3 to $12.7B–$13.3B and said data-center revenue should double again in 2027, yet shares still slid roughly 9% in extended trading despite the beat.

Why it matters: AMD is the clearest No. 2 read on AI-chip demand, and a strong beat that still gets sold echoes the AMD/SpaceX pattern this week — the bar for AI winners has risen from "growing fast" to "growing fast enough to justify the multiple."

📅 Aug 3, 2026

Snap posted Q2 revenue of $1.6B, up 19% and ahead of expectations, with daily active users up 5% to 493M and its net loss cut to $164M from $263M a year ago. Adjusted EBITDA of $250M crushed the $192M forecast, and the stock rose about 8% after hours on an upbeat current-quarter outlook.

Why it matters: After a rough stretch, Snap's beat and guidance suggest the digital-ad recovery is broadening beyond the giants — good news for any platform that lives on advertising.

Google opens Android to registered third-party app stores

📅 Jul 15, 2026

Following its court loss to Epic Games, Google began letting rival app stores that register with it distribute apps more easily, and from July 22 made Play Store listings (names, icons, screenshots) available to registered U.S. third-party stores for a $5,000 annual access fee. A smoother "Registered App Stores" install flow is due with a major Android release by year-end.

Why it matters: This is the most concrete crack yet in Android's app-distribution monopoly, giving Samsung, Amazon, Epic, and others a real technical path to compete with the Play Store on more equal footing.

Astronomers spot the solar system's first "three-headed" asteroid — and it has a moon

📅 Aug 3, 2026

New images from Arizona's Large Binocular Telescope suggest the asteroid (44) Nysa is a first-of-its-kind "trilobate" body — most likely a contact trinary of three connected components — orbited by a newly found 0.6-mile-wide moon, S/2026 (44) 1, at least 170 km out. Researchers say it looks unlike anything previously observed.

Why it matters: Oddball bodies like Nysa are living records of how the solar system's building blocks stuck together, and mapping their strange shapes sharpens both planetary-formation models and asteroid-deflection planning.

NASA and SpaceX on track to launch Crew-13 to the ISS on Sept. 12

📅 Aug 3, 2026

Mission managers confirmed the four-person Crew-13 is on schedule for a no-earlier-than Sept. 12 liftoff from Pad 40 aboard a Falcon 9. Commander Jessica Watkins and pilot Luke Delaney (NASA) will fly with mission specialists Josh Kutryk (Canadian Space Agency) and Sergey Teteryatnikov (Roscosmos) for a stay running into spring 2027, running research on heart disease, Parkinson's, and spaceflight crop production.

Why it matters: Crew-13 keeps NASA's accelerated ISS crew-rotation cadence on track and again pairs U.S., Canadian, and Russian crew members — a rare thread of continued space cooperation amid geopolitical strain.

NASA's Psyche returns Mars-flyby data and time-lapse video after its gravity-assist slingshot

📅 Jul 17, 2026

NASA released imagery and a time-lapse from Psyche's May 15 Mars flyby, when the spacecraft passed within about 2,860 miles of the surface and used the planet's gravity to bend its path — no propellant needed — toward the metal-rich asteroid Psyche. The team used the pass to test instruments ahead of the 2029 arrival.

Why it matters: Gravity assists let deep-space missions reach targets they otherwise couldn't, and the flyby data confirms Psyche is healthy and on course to make humanity's first up-close study of a likely exposed planetary core.
