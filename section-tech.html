Meta goes on trial as 29 states allege it built Facebook and Instagram to addict kids

📅 Aug 18, 2026

Opening arguments began this week in an Oakland federal court, where a coalition of 29 state attorneys general — led by California — accuse Meta of engineering its apps to hook teens and children while assuring parents they were safe. The case, which also alleges the company illegally collected kids' data in violation of COPPA, is expected to run about six weeks.

Why it matters: A loss could force changes to how "engaging" features are designed for minors and set precedent for the wider multidistrict fight over social-media harms.

Nvidia's H200 AI chips reach China — but at just 13% of the licensed cap

📅 Aug 20, 2026

After Washington's framework cleared roughly ten Chinese firms — Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance and JD.com among them — to buy up to 75,000 H200s each (with 25% of revenue routed to the US Treasury and chips screened on US soil), the first shipments finally landed, with ByteDance and Tencent reportedly receiving about 10,000 apiece. Volumes remain far below the ceiling as Beijing steers buyers toward domestic silicon.

Why it matters: The trickle shows how tightly both governments now control the AI-chip trade — and how hard China is pushing homegrown alternatives to Nvidia.

Nevada clears Tesla, Uber and Waymo to run up to 8,000 robotaxis

📅 Aug 20, 2026

The Nevada Transportation Authority unanimously approved commercial permits for all three companies in Clark County, home to Las Vegas — up to 5,000 driverless vehicles for Tesla and 1,000 each for Waymo and Uber (operating through Motional and Zoox) over the next year.

Why it matters: Vegas becomes one of the most crowded robotaxi battlegrounds yet, a real-world test of whether rival operators can scale autonomy in the same city at once.

Amazon makes its Alexa+ assistant free on Fire TV, no Prime required

📅 Aug 19, 2026

Amazon is auto-upgrading current Fire TV Sticks, the Fire TV Cube, its Ember TVs and compatible Hisense and Panasonic sets to the AI-powered Alexa+, dropping the $19.99/month fee non-Prime users faced. It adds conversational search — ask for a "top-rated thriller," then narrow to "something newer with a female lead" — plus smart-home control, with no app or subscription to enroll in.

Why it matters: Putting a capable assistant on tens of millions of TVs for free is a land-grab for the living-room interface — and fresh pressure on Google TV and Roku.

Apple rushes out iOS 26.6.1 to plug a zero-click image bug Meta found

📅 Aug 18, 2026

Apple patched CVE-2026-65346, an integer-overflow flaw in its ImageIO framework that could run malicious code when a device merely processes a booby-trapped image — a classic zero-click spyware vector. Fixes shipped across iOS/iPadOS 26.6.1, macOS Tahoe and the older iOS 18.7.10, crediting a researcher on Meta's Red Team X.

Why it matters: Image-parsing bugs are prime targets for commercial spyware, so users should update promptly even though Apple hasn't confirmed active exploitation.

🔬 Science & Emerging
Notable

Webb spots a "black hole star," a brand-new type of cosmic object

📅 Aug 12, 2026

MIT-led astronomers using the James Webb Space Telescope found MoM-BH*-1, an object from roughly 660 million years after the Big Bang that looks like a colossal star but is actually a growing black hole cloaked in a dense hydrogen envelope, radiating far more energy than any star could produce. The result, published in Nature, may explain how billion-solar-mass black holes formed so early.

Why it matters: A new class of object could resolve the puzzle of the early universe's mysterious "little red dots" and how supermassive black holes grew so fast.

Rocket Lab launches a Japanese radar-imaging satellite on its 93rd Electron flight

📅 Aug 21, 2026

Electron lifted off from New Zealand on the "Lightning God Defends" mission, deploying iQPS's latest QPS-SAR synthetic-aperture-radar satellite to a 575 km orbit — Rocket Lab's 14th launch of the year and the ninth in a booked run building out the Japanese Earth-observation constellation by 2030.

Why it matters: Small-satellite radar constellations are making all-weather, day-or-night Earth imaging cheaper and far more frequent for climate, defense and disaster response.
