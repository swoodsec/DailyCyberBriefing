Europe's first total solar eclipse since 1999 arrives Aug 12

📅 Aug 1, 2026

On August 12 a total solar eclipse will trace a narrow path across Siberian Russia, eastern Greenland, the west coast of Iceland, and northern Spain, with up to 2 minutes 18 seconds of totality offshore Iceland. It is the first total eclipse to cross mainland Europe since 1999, and the continent won't see another until 2075. The same new moon sets up an excellent dark-sky Perseid peak that night.

Why it matters: A rare, accessible eclipse over Europe is a once-in-a-generation event for millions of skywatchers — and a windfall for solar-physics researchers who chase the Sun's corona during totality.

Webb finds a hidden third giant planet in the Beta Pictoris system

📅 Jul 24, 2026

Astronomers using the James Webb Space Telescope discovered Beta Pictoris d, a giant planet lurking in one of the most-studied young planetary systems in our galaxy, making it only the second system known to host at least three directly detected planets. Unlike its siblings, Beta Pictoris d was found by the chemical fingerprint of its atmosphere rather than by direct imaging.

Why it matters: Detecting a planet through its atmospheric signature, not just its light, is a technique that could transform how astronomers uncover worlds hidden in the glare of their stars.
