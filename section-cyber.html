Critical

Clop launches data-theft extortion campaign against PTC Windchill and FlexPLM

📅 Jul 24, 2026

Clop (Cl0p) is exploiting CVE-2026-12569, a critical unauthenticated deserialization flaw in PTC's Windchill and FlexPLM product-lifecycle-management platforms, to deploy JSP webshells and exfiltrate product design data. ReliaQuest reports active exploitation across aerospace, automotive, defense and retail customers; Clop has begun emailing victims from new cryptohox.com extortion addresses, its long-standing pattern before publishing stolen files.

Why it matters: PLM platforms hold a company's actual product IP, not just business records — if you run Windchill or FlexPLM, patch immediately, place the servers behind a VPN or access gateway, and treat any suspected compromise as a design-theft incident, not just a data breach.

Critical

Russian "Laundry Bear" spies exploited Zimbra zero-click flaw for a year to steal mail and 2FA codes

📅 Jul 23, 2026

NSA, CISA and partner agencies confirmed that a Russian state-backed group (tracked as Laundry Bear, Void Blizzard, CL-STA-1114 and TA488) exploited CVE-2025-66376, a stored-XSS zero-click flaw in Zimbra's Classic UI, since at least July 2025 to steal 90 days of mail, browser-saved passwords, 2FA scratch codes and directory listings from government, defense and financial targets across NATO, Ukraine and the US. The payload also mints an app-specific password that survives a victim's password reset.

Why it matters: Patching Zimbra closes the hole but not the account — any mailbox that opened a matching message in a vulnerable session should have its password reset, sessions invalidated and 2FA scratch codes regenerated, since the app-specific credential the malware creates outlives a routine password change.

FBI/CISA update Iranian PLC-targeting advisory: Schneider and Siemens now in scope, active disruptions confirmed

📅 Jul 22, 2026

A joint FBI, CISA, NSA, EPA, DOE and Treasury advisory (AA26-097A) updated this week adds new indicators and expands its warning beyond Rockwell Automation to include Iranian-affiliated actors opportunistically targeting internet-exposed Schneider Electric and Siemens PLCs. Actors are downloading malicious project files that override safety shutdown and alarm logic and manipulating HMI/SCADA displays, causing operational disruption and financial loss at water, wastewater, energy and government-facility victims since March 2026.

Why it matters: The technique doesn't require a new vulnerability — it abuses internet-exposed PLCs reachable with the vendor's own legitimate engineering software, so the fix is exposure reduction: pull PLCs off the public internet behind a secure gateway and verify project-file logic against known-good backups.

Chick-fil-A confirms 13,322 customers affected by credential-stuffing breach

📅 Jul 24, 2026

Chick-fil-A's breach-notification filings with state attorneys general put a hard number on last week's disclosure: 13,322 people had names, emails, membership numbers, mobile-pay numbers and partial card digits exposed after automated credential-stuffing attacks hit Chick-fil-A One accounts between June 17 and 19. The company logged out affected accounts, removed stored payment methods and restored balances; MFA was not mandatory on the targeted accounts.

Why it matters: This is Chick-fil-A's second major credential-stuffing breach since 2023 — recurring incidents against the same account type are a signal that optional MFA isn't holding, and loyalty-program operators should treat default-on MFA as the actual fix, not a suggestion.

Critical

"Certighost" exploit lets any Active Directory user impersonate a Domain Controller

📅 Jul 24, 2026

Researchers H0j3n and Aniq Fakhrul published a working exploit for CVE-2026-54121, a flaw in an AD CS certificate-enrollment fallback ("chase") that lets a normal domain account obtain a Domain Controller certificate and authenticate as that machine via PKINIT — then pull the krbtgt secret through DCSync. Microsoft patched the flaw (CVSS 8.8) on July 14; exploitation needs only network access and a domain account, no admin rights or user interaction, and no evidence of in-the-wild exploitation had surfaced as of publication.

Why it matters: This is a full-domain-compromise path from an ordinary user account with a public working exploit now available — if you run an Enterprise CA, install Microsoft's July 14 update on AD CS hosts today rather than waiting for your normal patch cycle.

Autonomous AI pentester XBOW found Bing Images flaws letting crafted SVGs run as SYSTEM on Microsoft servers

📅 Jul 23, 2026

XBOW, an autonomous offensive-security platform, privately reported two critical, unauthenticated flaws (CVE-2026-32194, CVE-2026-32191, both CVSS 9.8) in Bing's "Search by Image" and reverse-image-crawler pipelines: a one-pixel SVG whose reference executed commands as NT AUTHORITY\SYSTEM on Microsoft's Windows image-processing workers, and as root on the Linux machines in the same fleet. Microsoft fixed both server-side back in March; XBOW published the mechanics July 23 at Microsoft's request, once remediation had fully landed.

Why it matters: No customer action is needed here, but the discovery method is the story — an autonomous AI system found and chained two unauthenticated RCE paths into a major cloud provider's production image pipeline without a human operator in the loop.

🤖 AI Security
Critical

Hacker runs open-source Hermes AI agent unattended for post-exploitation at Thai Finance Ministry

📅 Jul 24, 2026

An intruder already inside Thailand's Ministry of Finance network pointed the open-source Hermes AI agent at it with approval prompts disabled ("YOLO mode"), letting it autonomously run privilege-escalation scans, hunt for elevated-permission files, and crawl a personnel-records folder dating to 2012 — without a human approving each command. The operator left the agent's own logs on an open web directory, where Hunt.io and researcher Bob Diachenko found 585 files and 470MB of attack tooling, including custom kernel-exploit scripts and a Hadoop database service left accepting any password by default.

Why it matters: Unlike prior AI-assisted attacks where a vendor could ban the account, Hermes ran locally with no vendor watching and no kill switch — treat "AI agent in YOLO mode" as a distinct detection target and alert on web servers reaching internal Hadoop ports or a HermesWebUI response header, both of which this operator's setup exposed.

"AgentForger" flaw let one phishing link forge a persistent rogue agent inside ChatGPT Workspace

📅 Jul 23, 2026

Zenity Labs found that OpenAI's ChatGPT Agent Builder accepted an initialization prompt via URL parameter and auto-executed it, so a single crafted link — clicked by a logged-in employee with a connected app like Outlook or Slack — could silently create, configure with approvals switched off, and schedule an hourly-running AI agent under the victim's identity. The forged agent then took new instructions via emails tagged "TASK" from the attacker, harvesting documents and credentials and impersonating the victim in Teams phishing. OpenAI fixed the CSRF flaw June 8, before Zenity's public disclosure.

Why it matters: This is a cross-site request forgery attack that spawns an autonomous, self-scheduling AI insider rather than just stealing a session — audit which of your enterprise AI agent-builder tools accept state through URL parameters, since that pattern generalizes well beyond ChatGPT.

"Dolphin X" infostealer uses an AI profiler to rank which victims are worth robbing

📅 Jul 22, 2026

Varonis Threat Labs analyzed Dolphin X, a Windows stealer/RAT sold on a cybercrime forum for €69.95/month that targets 300+ applications — browsers, 100+ crypto-wallet extensions, SSH keys, .env files and cloud CLI tokens. Its "AI Profiler" scores each infected machine on app usage and browsing activity, returning operators a daily ranked summary so they can triage which of potentially thousands of victims to investigate first, plus an opt-in server-side mutation engine to evade hash-based detection.

Why it matters: Commodity infostealers are adopting the same AI-triage pattern seen in other 2026 malware (SpamGPT, Bluekit) — keep long-lived credentials and cloud tokens off disk in project directories, since infostealers grab everything in one pass and AI now helps attackers decide what to do with the haul first.

Google adds opt-in "selfie video" sign-in and account recovery with deepfake liveness checks

📅 Jul 23, 2026

Google launched an optional selfie-video verification method that captures guided head-movement video during setup, then compares a fresh video against it — with liveness detection meant to reject photos, replays and AI-generated deepfakes — to help users regain access when locked out. The encrypted video is deletable at any time and isn't offered for Workspace, child, or Advanced Protection accounts; the rollout lands alongside a separate Google Cloud hand-gesture reCAPTCHA aimed at automated bot traffic.

Why it matters: Biometric account-recovery is becoming a mainstream option precisely because deepfake-capable attackers are getting better at defeating older recovery flows (email/SMS) — worth tracking whether liveness detection holds up as an attack surface once it's at Google's user scale.

Microsoft mandates passkeys in Entra ID, retires SMS/voice MFA by February 2027

📅 Jul 22, 2026

Microsoft told Entra ID admins that SMS and voice will stop working as MFA methods on February 1, 2027, saying AI has "changed the economics of phishing" by automating fake sign-in pages, credential theft and SIM-swap operations at scale. Passkey registration prompts begin September 1, 2026; a temporary telecom-provider opt-out is available only through the transition window, and Microsoft says there is no extension past the February deadline for any tenant.

Why it matters: This is a hard enforcement date with no opt-out after February 2027 — run Microsoft's published PowerShell usage-analyzer now to find every account still on SMS/voice MFA in your tenant, since anyone left on it will be locked out of sign-in (not the account) until they register a passkey.
