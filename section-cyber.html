INC Ransomware becomes the dominant actor exploiting SonicWall SMA 1000 zero-days

📅 Aug 3, 2026

Resecurity reported over the weekend that INC Ransomware has accelerated activity since early August exploiting CVE-2026-15409 (CVSS 10) and CVE-2026-15410 in SonicWall SMA 1000 series VPN appliances, weaponized as zero-days since at least June 22 and patched by SonicWall in mid-July. Rapid7 found the attacks extract credentials, active session data and TOTP seeds for persistent access; the group has claimed 885 victims to date across the US, Australia, UAE, Colombia and Switzerland, with its most recent listing on August 2.

Why it matters: A patched vulnerability is still an active breach vector for anyone who hasn't rotated credentials — if you run SMA 1000 appliances, assume any session or TOTP seed active during the exposure window is compromised and force a full re-enrollment, not just a patch.

Suspected Iran-linked hackers hit water-utility PLCs across at least seven US states

📅 Aug 3, 2026

Following a July 26-27 attack on more than 30 Minnesota water and wastewater facilities, Michigan, South Dakota and Georgia have reported similar hostile activity against operational-technology systems, with U.S. investigators treating Iran-linked actors as the leading — but not yet confirmed — suspects. CISA said threat actors are targeting programmable logic controllers directly and changing passwords to lock operators out of their own equipment.

Why it matters: "Locking operators out of their own PLCs" is a physical-safety issue, not just a data one — water-sector IT teams should verify PLC default credentials have been changed and that remote-access interfaces aren't exposed to the internet, today, not on the next audit cycle.

"CaptiveCrunch": Russian APT29 sub-cluster hijacks hotel Wi-Fi to plant a surveillance RAT

📅 Aug 1, 2026

Microsoft attributes a campaign it tracks as CaptiveCrunch to Storm-2945, an operational sub-cluster of Midnight Blizzard (APT29/Cozy Bear), active since early May at hotels, conference centers and other shared-Wi-Fi venues. By gaining administrative control of the captive-portal gateway — which often doubles as the DNS resolver — the actor forges DNS answers to redirect victims to fake browser-update pages that install CornFlake, a Go-based RAT with webcam, microphone, keylogging and Microsoft 365/Azure AD token-theft capabilities.

Why it matters: Business travelers connecting to hotel Wi-Fi should treat any "update your browser" prompt on a captive portal as hostile by default — use a VPN on hotel networks and verify update prompts through the browser's own update mechanism, never a page served by the network itself.

N-central auth-bypass now in CISA's KEV catalog as attackers keep pivoting via Cloudflare tunnels

📅 Aug 3, 2026

CISA added CVE-2026-18577 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day N-able detailed how attackers who compromised N-central servers used the built-in Take Control feature to reach managed endpoints, then registered outbound Cloudflare tunnels as persistent services — requiring no inbound firewall rule to survive even after the N-central access path was revoked.

Why it matters: An outbound-only persistence mechanism won't show up in a normal inbound firewall review — audit managed endpoints for unrecognized Cloudflare tunnel services in addition to confirming the 2026.3.1.7 hotfix is installed.

"Pass-ta-key" attacks let Windows malware silently hijack Google-synced passkeys

📅 Aug 3, 2026

Unit 42 detailed three attack paths against Chrome's Google Password Manager cloud authenticator — Pass-ta-key, Silver Pass-ta-key and Golden Pass-ta-key — none of which break passkey cryptography itself. Each requires malware already running on a compromised Windows endpoint; the most severe, Golden Pass-ta-key, extracts the 32-byte Security Domain Secret that decrypts every passkey synced through a victim's account, letting an attacker authenticate from a completely different machine with no further access needed. Google removed the secret from logs after the report but Unit 42 says it remains temporarily readable in Chrome's process memory.

Why it matters: Passkeys were sold as phishing-proof, and cryptographically they still are — but this shows a compromised endpoint can extract and export the master secret protecting every synced passkey, so endpoint hygiene remains the actual control, not the authentication method.

CISA adds actively-exploited Fortinet FortiOS patch-bypass flaw to KEV catalog

📅 Aug 3, 2026

CISA added CVE-2025-68686 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog: a flaw letting an attacker who has already gained filesystem-level access to FortiOS bypass a prior patch meant to block a symbolic-link persistence technique, via specially crafted HTTP requests. It follows last week's KEV addition of the maximum-severity Arista VeloCloud command-injection bug (CVE-2026-16812). Federal agencies must apply mitigations by August 10.

Why it matters: This bug only matters to someone who's already inside your FortiOS box via a separate flaw — it's a persistence-hardening patch, not a perimeter one, so prioritize it alongside (not instead of) closing whatever gets an attacker onto the device in the first place.

18 malicious npm packages deliver a cross-platform RAT to Alibaba tool users

📅 Aug 3, 2026

Researchers uncovered a layered npm supply-chain cluster built around "lib-mtop," an unscoped package impersonating a private Alibaba "@ali"-scoped library; three new versions pushed in March and April 2026 turned a dormant three-year-old package into a downloader. Follow-on packages fetch a staged payload from Alibaba Cloud-hosted infrastructure that fingerprints the host and installs a full remote access trojan with a command set including execute, run_python, proxy and lateral-movement functions, targeting Chinese-speaking developer environments specifically.

Why it matters: A three-year-dormant package suddenly shipping new versions is a detectable pattern — if you use internal dependency scanning, flag any long-dormant package that resumes publishing, especially ones with names that shadow a scoped/private package you don't control.

Critical

"FaceHugger" flaws let malicious Hugging Face model repos bypass code-safety gate

📅 Aug 3, 2026

Zafran Labs researchers Gal Zaban and Ido Shani disclosed three flaws (CVE-2026-44827, CVE-2026-45804, CVE-2026-44513, up to CVSS 8.8) in Hugging Face's Diffusers library — used by over 8.1 million downloads in July alone — that bypass trust_remote_code, the safeguard meant to stop unreviewed code from running when loading custom pipelines. Each traces to a time-of-check-to-time-of-use flaw: the model download happens as two separate, non-atomic HTTP requests, and the security gate only checks the first. Diffusers 0.38.0 relocates the check to close the bypass.

Why it matters: With Hugging Face functioning as "the GitHub of the AI era," a bypassed trust gate in a library embedded across CI/CD pipelines and container images is a supply-chain risk at scale — audit which of your pipelines load custom Diffusers pipelines and confirm you're on 0.38.0 or later before pulling any new model repository.

Google's AI bug-hunting agent finds a 13-year-old Chrome sandbox-escape flaw

📅 Jul 31, 2026

An agent harness built around Gemini and used by Google's Chrome Security team since early 2026 found CVE-2026-3545 (CVSS 9.8), a sandbox-escape bug that let a compromised renderer trick the browser into reading local files — undetected for 13 years. Google says the harness helped it patch 1,072 Chrome vulnerabilities across versions 149 and 150 released in June, more than the total fixes from the prior 23 releases combined.

Why it matters: The same AI capability that's finding decade-old defensive bugs is available to attackers pointed at your own code — the record patching pace is good news only if your update cadence can keep up with a discovery pipeline running at machine speed.
