ShinyHunters leaks 7.1 million records stolen from Baxter International

📅 Aug 21, 2026

The extortion crew ShinyHunters published roughly 7.1 million records it says it lifted from the medical-device maker's Salesforce environment, including names, contact details and device purchase history. Baxter had earlier acknowledged "unauthorized activity" tied to third-party apps.

Why it matters: It is the latest in a wave of ShinyHunters thefts from corporate Salesforce tenants, showing that a single compromised SaaS app can expose millions of customer records.

📅 Aug 19, 2026

Healthcare technology company CareCloud told U.S. regulators that a data breach earlier this year affected more than 3.75 million people, making it one of the largest health-data incidents disclosed in 2026 and the first confirmation of the breach's scale.

Why it matters: Stolen medical records fuel identity theft and insurance fraud, and affected patients should watch for notification letters and monitor their accounts.

Pokémon Center customers exposed via CEVA Logistics breach

📅 Aug 18, 2026

Pokémon Center told shoppers in the UK and Germany that names, addresses, phone numbers, emails and order details leaked through a breach at third-party fulfillment partner CEVA Logistics, which also disrupted shipping and cancelled some orders. Payment card data was not affected.

Update

CISA adds exploited Zimbra mail-server flaw to KEV, sets Aug 24 deadline

📅 Aug 21, 2026

What's new: Beyond the earlier reports that attackers were exploiting this Zimbra Collaboration flaw, CISA has now formally added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog as CVE-2026-73570 and ordered federal agencies to patch by August 24. The bug lets an unauthenticated attacker run shell commands; a fix shipped in version 10.1.20.

Why it matters: A KEV listing with a binding deadline signals confirmed, ongoing exploitation — with roughly 12,000 Zimbra servers still exposed online, patching is urgent.
Source
Critical

Critical GitLab flaw exploited within days of emergency patch

📅 Aug 20, 2026

Attackers began exploiting CVE-2026-19478, a critical (CVSS 9.4) code-injection flaw in GitLab's GraphQL, roughly two days after disclosure. An unauthenticated attacker can modify or delete public projects and data without any user interaction; GitLab shipped emergency fixes (19.2.4 and earlier branches).

Why it matters: GitLab holds source code and CI/CD pipelines, so a wormable, no-login flaw is a prime target — self-managed installs should update immediately.
Source
High

CISA flags two actively exploited TrueConf Server flaws

📅 Aug 21, 2026

CISA added CVE-2026-72529 and CVE-2026-72530 in TrueConf Server to its KEV catalog after the Head Mare hacktivist group chained them to hijack on-premises instances and push PhantomCore malware to meeting participants. The flaws were patched in June (versions 5.3.9/5.4.9/5.5.5).

Why it matters: Attackers turned a video-conferencing server into a malware-delivery channel for everyone who joined a call — organizations still running older TrueConf builds should patch now.

Apple macOS, SharePoint and vCenter flaws join CISA's exploited list

📅 Aug 19, 2026

CISA ordered agencies to patch a fresh batch of actively exploited bugs, including a critical (CVSS 9.8) macOS Screen Sharing authentication-bypass (CVE-2026-65400), a Microsoft SharePoint weak-authentication flaw, and a Broadcom VMware vCenter path-traversal issue — all now confirmed under attack.

Why it matters: These are widely deployed enterprise platforms, and a KEV listing means the exploitation is real, not theoretical — apply vendor updates on priority.

"Zombie Card" attack revives expired Visa cards for contactless payments

📅 Aug 18, 2026

University of Massachusetts Amherst researchers showed that a relay attack can make expired Visa contactless cards complete real purchases, by rewriting an expiry field that Visa's contactless kernel does not cover with the card's digital signature. Equivalent Mastercard, Amex and Discover configs rejected the trick.

Why it matters: It undercuts the assumption that a lapsed or "cancelled" card is harmless, though the attack requires close physical proximity and specialized relay gear.

9,300+ leaked AWS keys still work — Hugging Face is the top source

📅 Aug 21, 2026

Truffle Security found that 88% of thousands of publicly exposed AWS credentials still authenticated, including 768 tied to companies with full admin rights and 526 root keys. AI model hub Hugging Face was the single biggest source, leaking 8,482 live keys across 3,394 public datasets, alongside Git histories, Docker images and CI/CD logs.

Why it matters: AI training data and model repositories have become a sprawling new place for secrets to leak — organizations should scan datasets and repos for credentials and rotate exposed keys.
Source
Medium

Nearly 800 malicious npm packages deliver cross-platform malware

📅 Aug 6, 2026

Researchers flagged a cluster of nearly 800 npm packages using AI-generated, typosquatted names to push a cross-platform remote-access trojan and infostealer. Unusually, the packages instruct developers to load them via require() rather than relying on install hooks, and target developer machines including AI coding-tool configurations.

Why it matters: Open-source package registries remain a favorite path onto developer laptops; teams should pin dependencies and vet unfamiliar packages before installing.
